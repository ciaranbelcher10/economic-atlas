Roadmap

What's next, and what's already been ruled in or out, updated as work happens, not as a promise of dates.

Roadmap

  1. Monetary policy dashboard: the Bank Rate vote record (previous and new rate, vote split, economic context, links to primary sources) is live on the UK Inflation tab. Remaining, parked for now: a "what changed" comparison against the previous statement (blocked on Bank of England copyright: it isn't Open Government Licensed, so a literal text diff isn't safely reproducible), market-implied rate expectations before and after (SONIA/OIS, feasibility not yet researched), and market reaction in gilts, sterling and the FTSE.
  2. Economic Health Score: a single composite score per country drawn from GDP growth, inflation, unemployment, debt, current account and productivity, for at-a-glance comparison.
  3. Timeline slider: drag through years on the front-page map and watch the figures update.
  4. Labour market breakdowns by age: 16-24 unemployment and the breakdown of why people are economically inactive are both live on the UK Labour tab, from real ONS data. Remaining: unemployment and inactivity for the other age bands (25-49, 50-64, 65+), currently shown as illustrative placeholders pending a fuller ONS series.
  5. Next countries: the site is currently too Western/major-economy-weighted for something calling itself an atlas, so expansion is now organised in tiers, global breadth first. Tier 1: Brazil, Morocco, Israel, South Africa and Mexico, covering South America, Africa, the Middle East and North America. Tier 2: a deeper dive into individual Eurozone/EU member countries (Germany, France, Italy, Spain and others) as their own pages rather than folded into a single Eurozone aggregate, ties directly to the still-open decision on demoting the Eurozone tab to a benchmark/context role. Tier 3: not yet decided. Throughout, still prioritising consistency and depth over sheer number of countries covered.
  6. China page: pending a reliable high-frequency data source; free Western mirrors of Chinese statistics are sparse, and stale data would break the freshness promise.
  7. Markets: FX, equity indices and gilt yields alongside the macro data.
  8. AI MPC meeting summaries: a structured, neutral extract of each Bank of England Monetary Policy Summary, minutes and (where one exists) press conference, reorganised into fixed factual categories with no commentary or opinion, clearly labelled as AI-generated, and reviewed before publishing. Parked for now: this is a genuine departure from the site's "data first, no commentary" principle, worth doing carefully rather than quickly.

Have an idea or want an indicator added? Suggest it; the roadmap is shaped by what readers ask for.
