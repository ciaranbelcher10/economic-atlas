The Economic Atlas

Markets

Government bond yields and exchange rates from official sources: central banks, finance ministries and national statistics offices. Every row shows the date of its latest figure and where it comes from. The light beside each country is green when the figure is as recent as its source normally allows, amber when it is overdue, and red when it is well overdue. Click a column heading to sort.

10-year government bond yields

The yield on each government's benchmark 10-year bond, as each source publishes it: for most, the average over the month, or the quarter where that is all the source publishes; where the period says end of month, it is the reading on the last trading day. Changes are in percentage points.

Currencies against the US dollar

How many US dollars one unit (or a round number of units) of each currency buys, from the Federal Reserve's daily H.10 rates, which it publishes weekly. A rise means a stronger currency, so a positive change is a gain against the dollar. Euro-area members share the euro, shown once.
