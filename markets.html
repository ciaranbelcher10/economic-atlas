Policy rates and the 10-year spread

The interest rate each central bank sets, beside the 10-year yield for the same country. The spread is the 10-year yield minus that rate, in percentage points, for the same month only, so it uses the rate in force that month, which can differ from the latest rate. Where the latest yield and the rate for that month are not both published, the spread shows n/a. Canada and Japan show a market overnight rate, which tracks the central bank's own rate closely but is not the rate it announces. China shows the one-year loan prime rate, the benchmark bank lending rate, not the seven-day reverse repo rate the People's Bank of China uses for its operations. Countries without a stored policy rate are not listed.
