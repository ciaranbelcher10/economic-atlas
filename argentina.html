    On the roadmap: federal debt stock, and a breakdown of where public money goes.

    Data: FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (IMF, OECD), the OECD and the World Bank. Series codes are shown with each metric; figures are the latest published observations and are subject to revision. A policy interest rate series, current account balance, and labour force participation aren't currently wired up for Argentina -- no verified live FRED/OECD source was found for any of them. CPI now pulls directly from INDEC (Argentina's national statistics office) as the primary source, with OECD's live prices system as a fallback if that fails -- this is new as of this update and hasn't been confirmed against a real run yet, so if the inflation chart above looks wrong or is missing, that's the first thing to check. Separately, Argentina's official CPI figures from 2007-2015 (INDEC) are widely regarded by independent economists and the IMF as having understated true inflation during that period; data since a 2016 methodology overhaul is generally considered credible again. This isn't a data-pipeline gap, it's a note on the source itself -- included here rather than presented without context. These are real, disclosed gaps, not an oversight.
